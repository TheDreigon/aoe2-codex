Germans

Who they were

From Henry I's election (919) to 1500, the codex's Germans represent the East Frankish, later German, kingdom. From 962 it was the core of the Empire, called "Holy Roman" only from 1254. Its lands reached from both banks of the Rhine and the Alps to the North Sea and the Baltic; settlers, townspeople and miners spread beyond them, and the Teutonic Order, recruited mainly in the Empire, ruled much of Livonia and, until 1525, Prussia. The game's "Teutons" recalls an ancient people beaten by Rome in 102 BC, whose name medieval writers revived as Teutonici, a learned equivalent of theodiscus (deutsch). Italians and popes spoke of a regnum Teutonicorum; from the eleventh century the kings styled themselves kings of the Romans.

Rise and height

Henry I (919–936), elected at Fritzlar by Franks and Saxons only, made Swabia and Bavaria submit by 921 and beat the Magyars at Riade in 933. Under Otto I (936–973) their raids on the West ended at the Lechfeld (955), and in 962 he was crowned emperor in Rome (Reuter 1991). The Salian Henry IV clashed with Pope Gregory VII over the appointment of bishops, a quarrel settled in 1122. The Staufen Frederick Barbarossa conceded the Lombard cities self-government within the Empire at Constance (1183) and died on crusade in 1190; Henry VI won Sicily in 1194. The Teutonic Order, by its own account founded as a field hospital at Acre in 1190, began conquering Prussia in 1230. After 1250 rival kings competed until Rudolf of Habsburg's election (1273); in 1356 Charles IV's Golden Bull codified the royal election by seven electors and granted them rights over the mines in their lands. Meanwhile the Hanse, a loose association of towns headed by Lübeck, dominated northern trade and, with allies, defeated Denmark (1367–70).

War and society

The chronicler Widukind reports that, during a truce with the Magyars, Henry I made one in nine of the agrarii milites ("rural soldiers", a debated term) live in forts storing a third of the harvest. Castles and walled towns multiplied later, mostly from the eleventh century on. In the Indiculus loricatorum (981), most of the mailed warriors summoned to Otto II in Italy were owed by bishoprics and abbeys. From the eleventh century kings, bishops and princes relied on ministeriales, legally unfree knights; by the later twelfth century these formed most of the German knighthood (Arnold 1985). Settlement agents, from the twelfth century, brought peasants east of the Elbe under "German law", with rent-free first years and the heavy plough (Bartlett 1993). German miners brought their skills to Bohemia and Hungary, and "Saxon" miners from Hungary to Serbia by the mid-thirteenth century. The Order's knight-brothers, bound by monastic vows, fought as heavy cavalry, leading sergeants, crusading guests and Prussian light horsemen (Christiansen 1997). Fifteenth-century Nuremberg and Augsburg were leading armour-making centres, Gutenberg printed with movable type at Mainz from about 1450, and from about 1486 Maximilian I raised Landsknechte, pike infantry on the Swiss model.

End and legacy

Around 1500 the Empire was decentralised but working: the Diet of Worms (1495) proclaimed a perpetual public peace and created a supreme court, and the Golden Bull remained fundamental law until 1806 (Wilson 2016). The Order fared worse: beaten by Poland and Lithuania at Grunwald (1410), it lost western Prussia to Poland in 1466, and in 1525 its Grand Master made the rest a secular duchy under Polish overlordship. The eastward settlement left lasting German-speaking communities across East Central Europe, and printing spread across Europe within a generation.

In the codex

The design reads the eastward settlement into Farms cost -40%, the forts and castles into Town Centers +10 garrison capacity; Towers +5 garrison capacity, the armourers into Infantry and Mounted Units +1/+2 melee armor in Castle/Imperial Age, religious vows and the Church's contingents into Units more resistant to conversion, and the Order's hospital into Monks +100% healing range; the miners give the team bonus, Natural gold sources last +15% longer. The Teutonic Knight rides like the brethren; the Landsknecht is Maximilian's pike infantry. The unique techs are the Ministerialen, the Golden Bull (the electors' mines), the Hanse and Gutenberg's Press.

design notes and sources

Contested

  • When the kingdom became "German": some historians start with the Treaty of Verdun (843) or the elections of 911 and 919, while others see a German kingdom and identity forming only gradually, from the eleventh century (Reuter 1991).
  • How Ottonian armies fought: the view Karl Leyser set out in the 1960s gives the decisive role to heavily armed horsemen (Leyser 1982); David Bachrach sees armies dominated by foot soldiers and siege warfare, built around fortifications and their garrisons in a Carolingian tradition (Bachrach 2012).
  • The late medieval Empire: nineteenth-century national historians, above all the "Prussian school", saw fragmentation and failure; more recent work sees a decentralised but functioning political system (Wilson 2016).
