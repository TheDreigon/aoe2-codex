# aoe2-codex rules

Version 0.5, 15 October 2026, amended 18 October 2026 (changes in section 12). This document is the project's constitution: everything we do to the civilizations must follow it. Each decision has a status:

- **[decided]**: agreed; changes only after a new discussion.
- **[in principle]**: accepted, but the details are still open.
- **[proposal]**: still to be discussed.

## 1. Goal

Show, on a site similar to aoe2techtree.net, a version of Age of Empires II in which every civilization has bonuses, units, technologies, a tech tree and a history text that are more faithful to history. The site also shows the original and explains every change, with sources.

For every civ the site also shows the time window it represents and, for renamed civs, the original name. **[decided]**

This is a non-commercial fan project. We are not trying to convince the developers; we do it for fun and for other history and AoE2 fans.

## 2. Scope

- Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition base game only. Age of Empires I, Return of Rome and Chronicles are out. **[decided]**
- Reference time frame: **500–1500**. A civ may touch the edges, but most of its existence, or the peak the game represents, must fall inside it. **[decided]**
- **Late elements.** An element that originated inside the time frame is fine even if its fame came later (the Turtle Ship is first recorded in 1413). An element that originated after 1500 is allowed only in the Imperial Age, at most one per civ, and only if it passes the relevance test with clear fame (the Tanegashima arquebus, 1543). **[decided]**

## 3. What a civ is

> A civ is a people or polity with its own identity, tied to a territory (or, for nomads, a defined grazing range), that lasted about two centuries, mostly within 500–1500, and whose way of fighting and living is documented well enough to design it without inventing. **[decided]**

The five tests:

1. **Identity.** It is a people or a state. Not a faction in a civil war, a passing dynasty or an activity.
2. **Continuity.** About two centuries (six to eight generations). What counts is the continuity of the people, not of the empire: the united Mongol Empire lasted only a few decades, but the Mongols exist before and after it.
3. **Territory.** A core territory or a defined nomadic range.
4. **Time frame.** Within 500–1500, as in section 2.
5. **Distinctiveness and sources.** It fought or lived in a documented way that differed from its neighbours, and there are sources (written or archaeological) to show it.

"Civilization" in the strict academic sense (urban, literate societies) would exclude peoples such as the Tupi, the Mapuche or the Cumans. In this project, "civ" means **a playable people**.

## 5. Rework levels

The level measures the **implementation cost for a future modder**, not historical importance. **[decided]**

| Level | What fits | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **N1: Redistribute** | Change which civ has which existing unit, tech or building (including other civs' unique ones). Rewrite bonuses using effects that already exist. Rename civs, units and techs. Reassign existing architecture sets. | Every effect must already exist somewhere in the game. Data and text only. |
| **N2: Adjust** | N1, plus: carefully change effects and costs of generic techs; add or remove a few generic techs; new unique units and techs built from mechanics that already exist; split lines whose graphics already exist (for example bow and crossbow). | Only mechanics the engine already supports, with no new art required. |
| **N3: Rebuild** | New buildings, units and mechanics; changes to the rock-paper-scissors counter system; new architecture sets; new civs. | Full freedom, but every change cites a source. |

On the site, every change carries its level label.

**Existing content includes the Scenario Editor.** Units that exist in the game, with art and data, but are trainable only in campaigns and the Scenario Editor (the Crusader Knight, the Mounted Samurai, the Pagan Priest, the Canoe and others, listed in [`tools/scenario-units.md`](tools/scenario-units.md), with their base stats in [`tools/scenario-units-stats.md`](tools/scenario-units-stats.md)) count as existing game content. A design may use one as the model of a unique unit, or as the look of a renamed generic unit. Each use passes the usual historical review; such a unit is chosen only where it fits a civ better than the current borrowing. **[decided]**

**Reusing a unit's art.** The model (art) of any existing unit, whether trainable in normal games or only in campaigns and the Scenario Editor, may be reused for a codex unit (owner's decision, 15 October 2026). **[decided]**

| Reuse | Level |
|---|---|
| The unit as it is, with only a new name | N1 |
| A new name and adapted stats (hit points, attack, armour, cost, speed and the like): a new unit without new art | N2 |
| New art or animations | N3 |

**Narrowing.** A passive bonus or team bonus that applies an existing effect to a narrower set of targets, or at a lower magnitude, is N1. A unique tech stays N1 only when it is identical in play to an existing tech (rule 6.5). **[decided]**

## 6. Civ design rules

1. **Composition.** Every civ has:
   - **4 to 6 passive bonuses**, with at least one economic and one military bonus. Unique buildings ("Can build X") and starting bonuses ("Start with X") count as passive bonuses. **[decided]**
   - **2 to 4 unique technologies.** One at the **Castle** and one at the **Town Center** are mandatory. Up to two more can be researched at other main buildings: Castle, Town Center, Market, Monastery, University, Dock, Mill, Lumber Camp, Mining Camp, Barracks, Archery Range, Stable, Siege Workshop. **[decided]**
   - **1 team bonus.** Whether some civs may have two is still open. **[proposal]**
   - **1 or 2 unique units.** A naval unique unit counts. Regional units (Steppe Lancer, Genitour/Jinete, Mounted Crossbowman) do not. **[in principle]**
   - **A unit that only one civ has is a unique unit**, wherever it is trained (also at the Archery Range or the Stable): it counts toward the 1 or 2, and the site colours it as one. A regional unit is one that two or more civs share. **[decided]** (owner, 18 October 2026)
   - A combined cap of **passive bonuses + unique techs ≤ 9**, so that no civ has both the most bonuses and the most techs. **[proposal]**
2. **Where each historical trait goes.** Ask these questions in order: **[decided]**
   1. Did it benefit neighbours or allies (trade, transferred techniques, mercenaries, alliances, religious influence)? Then it is the **team bonus**. A trait that only benefited the civ itself is never a team bonus.
   2. Is it **structural** (geography, environment, economic base, social structure, a long military tradition)? Then it is a **passive bonus**.
   3. Is it **datable** (a reform, invention, institution or adopted technique, usually with a proper name)? Then it is a **unique technology**: military or royal at the **Castle**; state or government at the **Town Center**; other themes at the building where they belong (trade at the Market, religion at the Monastery, knowledge and writing at the University, the sea at the Dock, farming at the Mill, and so on).
   4. The size of the effect only breaks ties: a very strong effect suits a tech (its cost and delay act as a brake); a small, continuous edge suits a passive. History decides first.
3. **Number of datable moments.** Extra unique techs exist only for distinct datable moments, never one reform split in two. If a civ has more moments than slots, the rest become age-gated passives ("starting in the Imperial Age") or N2 ideas. If it has too few, a structural trait with a recognisable moment of formalisation can be promoted (the Castilian municipal militias, formalised as the Santa Hermandad in 1476). For peoples without a state, the Town Center tech represents a community institution; if its historical basis is weak, we say so. **[in principle]**
4. **Ages of unique techs.** With two techs at the Castle, one is Castle Age and one Imperial Age, in chronological order within the civ's own time window. Techs at the Town Center and at civil buildings: Feudal Age at the earliest, never Dark Age. Military techs outside the Castle (Barracks, Archery Range, Stable, Siege Workshop, Dock): Castle Age at the earliest. **[in principle]**
5. **Levels of unique techs.** Moving an existing unique tech to another building, or renaming it, is N1. Creating a new tech, even with an effect that already exists elsewhere, is N2. **[in principle]**
6. **Unique technologies never just give things back.** A unique tech must never merely give back something the tree took away. If it unlocks something generic, it must also bring a second, exclusive effect. **[decided]**
7. **Repeated bonuses.** Allowed when history justifies them. A civ's identity comes from the combination of its bonuses, not from each bonus being unique. **[decided]**
8. **Grounding.** Every element (bonus, unit, tech, tree change) states its historical basis and, whenever possible, a source. Where historians disagree, we say so. **[decided]**
9. **Relevance test.** Every bonus, team bonus, unique unit and unique tech must pass this test: *if we erased this trait from the civ's real history, would its course or its identity change?* If not, the trait is irrelevant and does not deserve a special slot, however well documented it is. Avoid traits that are only details (one arrow type, one architectural feature, one battle anecdote) when the civ has weightier traits available. Historical weight counts most; fame counts too, but less. Something unknown to most players can pass on weight alone (the Mesta, the Inca *qullqa* storehouses, the Tuntian colonies). Fame can tip a borderline case, because a trait that people associate with the civ is part of its identity, but fame alone does not justify a slot. The higher the slot (team bonus, unique unit, unique tech), the higher the bar. If a civ runs short of relevant traits, prefer repeating a broad, well-founded bonus (rule 7) to using an obscure detail. **[decided]**
10. **Rename or replace.** Rename when the function has a historical equivalent (Horse Collar → *Chinampas* for the Aztecs). Replace or remove when it doesn't: a renamed trebuchet is still a trebuchet. Renames can be per civ. **[decided]**
11. **Replace rather than only remove.** When we remove something the civ needs in order to play, we look for a substitute with the same function (as the Traction Trebuchet does). **[in principle]**
12. **Consistency with the text.** The tree must not contradict our history text for the civ. **[in principle]**
13. **Civ profile.** Every civ has a profile with the entity it represents, its region, its time window and its main sources. **[in principle]**
14. **Game structure.** We keep the AoE2 skeleton: four ages, the counter system and a similar "power budget" across civs. N1 does not promise balance, but it avoids obvious absurdities. **[in principle]**
15. **Architecture sets.** Reassigning an existing set is N1 (Persians → Central Asian); creating a new set is N3 (a "Steppe" set for Avars, Mongols and Cumans). **[decided]**
16. **Codex-wide conventions.** The level-1 review (October 2026) settled 26 codex-wide questions (CQ1–CQ26). They are decided conventions for every civ; the owner can overrule any of them (section 11). **[decided]**
    1. **Navy (CQ1).** Navies are classified by vessel, not by water. Planked war galleys or ships make a **fleet of ships**, which keeps every generic tier. Dugouts, rafts and light boats make the **boat tier**, which keeps the generic lines without the Galleon and Carrack. A civ without a war fleet of its own keeps the **basic navy** (galleys, fire ships, the Hulk line, demolition ships and the Dock techs), without the Galleon, Carrack, Fast Fire Ship or a siege warship. Special warships (Cannon Galleon, Catapult Galleon, Dromon, Longship, Lou Chuan, Turtle Ship) appear only where the type is documented, and the Cannon Galleon needs gun-armed warships inside the window. Every file names its tier and the vessels it rests on.
    2. **Generic post-1500 items (CQ2).** The Petard, demolition ships, Heated Shot and the Galleon of a fleet of ships stay as flagged abstractions of the game's skeleton and never count as a civ's one late element (section 2).
    3. **Crossbows (CQ3).** In Old World civs the Crossbowman is the generic Castle Age bow tier, as an abstraction, until the bow and crossbow lines are split in N2. The Arbalester needs crossbow evidence. The pre-contact Americas have neither.
    4. **Thumb Ring (CQ26).** Only where the thumb draw is attested for the civ's archers, or for an archery tradition its armies demonstrably used (steppe, Islamic, East Asian).
    5. **Siege engines (CQ7, CQ13).** Engines that never reached a civ are removed; the counterweight Trebuchet appears in the 1160s–1180s and reached China only in 1273. The Mangonel line is the light stone-thrower everywhere. The Traction Trebuchet is given wherever heavy stone-throwing engines are attested before the counterweight era (the traction test). A documented transfer of engines meets the test (the Japanese: Goguryeo's gift of 618); an inference from a civ's wars and its neighbours' engines is admitted only when flagged as such (the Vietnamese, the Armenians). The two engines fill one Imperial Age slot, so a design whose window spans both eras keeps one of them and says why: the engine of the period its siege evidence falls in, or of its core where the evidence spans both eras (the Byzantines and Chinese keep the traction engine; the Bulgarians and Mongols the counterweight engine). Leaving out a counterweight Trebuchet that did reach a civ is then a game-role choice, not a historical finding. A civ may have no long-range anti-building weapon when none is attested. The American civs have no siege engines (owner's decision).
    6. **Americas (CQ12, CQ20).** The American civs keep the removals of siege engines, crossbows, the Halberdier and the Galleon. Blast Furnace, Plate Mail Armor and Ring Archer Armor stay as generic equipment levels, renamed where a documented equivalent exists, on the same footing as Forging, Scale Mail and Chain Mail. Renames of generic items follow rule 10 wherever a file documents the real equivalent, Coinage included; an item kept without an equivalent is flagged as an abstraction.
    7. **Mule Cart (CQ4).** It stays with its four holders (Armenians, Georgians, Ethiopians, Tatars); extending it waits for N2 or N3 (section 10).
    8. **Architecture (CQ5).** Historical fit decides. The steppe civs keep stopgap sets until a Steppe set exists (N3).
    9. **Religious vocabulary (CQ6).** The Monk, the Monastery and its techs keep the game's generic names in N1; the site notes that "Monk" fits Muslim civs worst.
    10. **Regional units (CQ8, CQ23).** No unit the game classifies as regional counts as a unique unit. The regional Genitour serves the Berbers, Castilians, Portuguese and Lithuanians (owner's decision), not the Malians.
    11. **Magnitudes and ages (CQ9, CQ10).** The ceiling is measured per unit class: no unit line goes beyond the highest value the game gives that class. An existing effect that is moved or becomes a passive keeps the game's age or a later one, never an earlier one; the site labels this as a game constraint.
    12. **Reuse (CQ11).** Reused effects are accepted in N1, each resting on its own history (rule 7). Identical team-wide effects count once per team, and trade-gold team bonuses count as one at the highest value present. A civ's own bonus and an ally's team bonus with the same effect stack as the game stacks them (for example relic gold for the Muisca with a Tatar ally); N1 accepts these stacks (rule 14), and the README of `civs/` lists them. A diversification pass for team bonuses and Town Center techs is planned for N2, and it revisits the stacks.
    13. **Power floor (CQ14).** No blanket compensation for kits that history leaves weak; only historically grounded extras.
    14. **Alliances of autonomous polities (CQ15).** Alliances between autonomous polities of one people (the Mapuche, the Tupi) are alliances between independent allies and satisfy rule 2.
    15. **Names (CQ16, CQ17, CQ18, CQ22).** A civ is renamed only where standard English scholarship does not use the game's name for the design's entity (section 4). A shared generic item is renamed for one civ only when it has the same function inside the window, the new name is a period term, and the generic name is foreign or anachronistic to the civ; modern coinages are flagged. A codex unit may take the name of a different existing game unit (Ghulam, Teutonic Knight, Houfnice), with a note on the site. Item names use a plain romanization for non-Latin scripts (Pinyin without tones, Revised Romanization, no scholarly diacritics for Arabic, Persian and Indic); Latin-script languages keep their diacritics.
    16. **Labels (CQ19).** The in-game label follows the redesign, using the game's label categories.
    17. **Windows past 1500 (CQ21).** A window may run to a terminal event soon after 1500, with a stated core inside the time frame.
    18. **Timurid Siegecraft (CQ24).** Not for the Persians; its best home is a possible Mongol Huihui Pao (1273), a design option.
    19. **Rule readings (CQ25).** (a) The chronological order of rule 4 binds only the two Castle techs. (b) An existing unique tech that renders a long tradition stays a tech only as a flagged fallback. (c) Defence lines go to the Castle, fortified towns to the Town Center. (d) The tree follows the period the design represents, not everything attested at the edge of the window.

## 7. History texts

- We write **our own texts**, with sources, flagging what is contested. **[decided]**
- We **do not republish** the game's original texts, which belong to Microsoft. For working purposes they stay local, in `codex/.local/original-history/` (ignored by git). **[decided]**
- The critical analysis of the original texts (claim, problem, correction) is in `codex/analysis/`.

## 10. Ideas kept for N2 and N3

- Split Archer and Crossbowman into two lines (N2: the graphics already exist).
- Blacksmith granularity: keep metallurgy shared and add technique techs per arm (couched lance for cavalry, pike drill for infantry).
- Defensive techs: boiling water and pitch, moat, talus (sloped wall base).
- A Samurai that switches between bow and sword (like the Ratha); javelins that reduce pierce armour (the *pilum* effect).
- No siege engines for the American civs; substitutes such as fire (Cuzco, 1536) and assault.
- Mule Cart extended to other pack-animal civs (Portuguese *almocreves*, Castilian *arrieros*); different animals (llamas, camels, yaks) are N3.
- A Dark Age that is less of a "hunting simulator": war bands and raids. Reconsider the name "Dark Age".
- Using Houses as walls is a gameplay exploit; fixing it requires engine changes (N3).
- A "Steppe" architecture set (N3).

## 11. Who decides

- **Historical questions** (what is true, how strong the evidence is, which reading of a contested point the design follows) are settled by Claude, the project's research assistant. Claude cites sources, flags contested points and runs an adversarial check on major calls. **[decided]**
- **Design, scope, priorities and taste** are settled by the project owner, TheDreigon, who can overrule any decision, historical ones included. **[decided]**
