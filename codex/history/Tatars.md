# Tatars

## Who they were

The codex's Tatars are the Ulus of Jochi (*ulus*: a people and its lands), the Mongol Empire's western realm, ruled by heirs of Chinggis Khan's eldest son. It runs from 1242 to 1502, with its core in 1242–1419. A Mongol elite ruled towns from Crimea to northern Khwarazm, south of the Aral Sea, and the Turkic-speaking Kipchak (Cuman) nomads of the Desht-i Qipchaq ("Kipchak steppe"), north of the Black and Caspian seas. Rus' princes paid tribute. "Golden Horde" is a sixteenth-century Russian name; contemporaries said Ulus of Jochi, Ulugh Ulus ("Great State") or, in Rus', the Horde. "Tatar", first the name of a confederation in eastern Mongolia that Chinggis destroyed in 1202, was applied by outsiders to the Mongols and later to the Horde's Turkic-speaking heirs. Unlike the game, the codex places Timur (Tamerlane), the Horde's enemy, with the Persians.

## Rise and height

Jochi's son Batu led the western campaign of 1236–1242, which crushed Muslim Volga Bulgaria and the Kipchaks, took Vladimir (1238) and Kiev (1240) and ravaged, without conquering, Poland and Hungary (1241–1242). He then withdrew to the lower Volga, where he later founded Sarai. Berke (1257–1266), the first Muslim khan, allied with Mamluk Egypt against the Mongol Ilkhans of Iran. The Horde peaked under Özbeg (1313–1341) and his son Janibeg (1342–1357). After 1359 khans came and went. In the west the beglerbeg (commander-in-chief) Mamai ruled through puppet khans until Dmitry of Moscow beat him at Kulikovo (1380); then Tokhtamysh, a descendant of Jochi backed by Timur, ousted him. Tokhtamysh reunited the Horde and sacked Moscow (1382), then turned on Timur, who crushed him on the Kondurcha (1391) and the Terek (1395) and sacked Sarai, Astrakhan and Tana. Edigu, a beg (noble) of the Manghit tribe, made and unmade khans, beat Vytautas of Lithuania at the Vorskla (1399) and besieged Moscow (1408).

## War and society

The khans' armies were Kipchak horse archers under Mongol princes and begs; the conquerors had intermarried with the Kipchaks until they seemed one people (al-Umari c. 1340). The papal envoy Carpini, crossing Batu's lands in 1246–1247, describes richer warriors in armour (Mongol and Turkic *quyaq*) of laced plates of hardened hide or iron (Carpini 1247). Battles turned on archery and feigned flight, as at the Vorskla. In 1344 an Italian relief force destroyed Janibeg's siege engines outside Genoese Caffa.

Herds of sheep, horses and cattle fed the nomads on mutton and kumis (fermented mare's milk), and the friar Rubruck saw felt tents carried whole on ox-carts (Rubruck 1255). The traveller Ibn Battuta rode in the wagons called *araba* and likened Özbeg's camp, the *ordu* (origin of "horde"), to a moving city with mosques and bazaars (Ibn Battuta 1355). The khans' charters (*yarlyks*) made and unmade Rus' princes; in time Moscow's grand princes collected the Rus' tribute for the khan (Halperin 1985). The *tamga*, a trade tax, was levied at Sarai and at Caffa in Crimea and Tana, where Genoese and Venetians traded (Favereau 2021). The khans spared the Church of Rus' the tribute in return for prayers, and an Orthodox bishop sat at Sarai from 1261; Özbeg made Islam the state religion.

## End and legacy

After Edigu's death (1419) the Horde gradually broke up: Kazan, Crimea and Astrakhan became khanates, Edigu's Manghits formed the Nogai Horde, and the Great Horde kept Sarai and its claim to the Rus' tribute. Its khan Ahmed failed to cross the Ugra against Ivan III of Moscow (1480), and in 1502 Mengli Giray of Crimea sacked Sarai and ended it. Volga and Crimean Tatars, Nogais, Kazakhs and Uzbeks partly descend from the Horde's peoples, and Russian *tamozhnya* (customs) comes from *tamga*.

## In the codex

Herds, Kipchak horsemen, steppe archery and wagon camps give *Livestock animals last +50% longer*, *Cavalry Archers cost -10/20% in Castle/Imperial Age*, *Thumb Ring, Parthian Tactics free* and *Can build Arabas*; the charters that spared the Church give the team bonus *Relics generate +33% gold*. The *Keshik*, named after Chinggis Khan's guard, which the Horde probably kept, renders the khans' armoured riders, and *Yarlyk*, *Tamga* and *Quyaq* the tribute, the trade tax and the armour. The man-powered *Traction Trebuchet* replaces the counterweight *Trebuchet*, and Timur's *Flaming Camel* and *Timurid Siegecraft* leave, as argued in the [design notes and sources](https://github.com/TheDreigon/aoe2-codex/blob/master/codex/civs/Tatars.toml).

## Contested

- **Plague or Timur.** How far the Black Death, rather than civil war and Timur, ruined the Horde is debated. Schamiloglu (2017) argues that the depopulation it caused from 1346 broke the succession system after 1359 and led to urban decline, though he grants that much of the evidence is indirect.
- **Corpses at Caffa.** Gabriele de' Mussis, a Piacenza notary who never claimed to have been at Caffa, wrote that the plague-stricken besiegers catapulted corpses into the town in 1346. Wheelis (2002) judged this plausible as the way plague entered Caffa, though probably not decisive for its spread to Europe; Zanders (2021) argues that none of the writers he reviews showed the besiegers had engines able to throw a body over the walls.
- **The "Tatar yoke".** Much Russian and Soviet scholarship saw Horde rule as a yoke of destruction; Halperin (1985) and Ostrowski (1998) stress instead what Muscovy borrowed from it in war and government.
