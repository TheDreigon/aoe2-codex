# Open questions for the owner

Every design, scope, priority and taste question that the Level 1 designs leave to the project owner ([RULES.md section 11](../RULES.md#11-who-decides)), collected from the design files. Historical questions are settled in the files themselves, with sources and contested notes. The codex-wide questions (CQ1, CQ2, ...) are listed in the [README](README.md#codex-wide-questions); the per-civ questions below often point to them.


## Britain and France

### Britons ([Britons.toml](Britons.toml))

1. Name (not decided; to be settled with the other open renames, codex-wide question CQ16): the design is England 1066–1485, and ‘Britons’ historically means the Brythonic peoples the English displaced (Welsh, Cornish, Bretons). Rename to ‘English’? If so, should the Saxons become ‘Anglo-Saxons’ to keep the two English civs distinct, and should ‘Britons’ be reserved for a future Brythonic civ?
2. Range: the history settles the direction (the longbow’s edge lay in rate of shooting and massed use, not range; see contested), so the only choice is how much range to keep for its fame. The civ stacks it twice, in the passive and in Yeomen. Options: (a) keep both, the game’s convention and the current default; (b) keep range once, on Yeomen (a level-1 tech whose effect cannot change), and turn the passive into rate of fire, ‘Foot Archers attack +10/+20% faster in Castle/Imperial Age’ (precedent: ‘Foot Archers attack +18% faster’, Ethiopians). Option (b) is the one that follows the history. Which?
3. Warwolf is dropped under the relevance test (a single engine at a single siege) and its Castle slot goes to the Chevauchée. It is the Britons’ best-known tech in the game: keep it anyway for its fame, either instead of the Chevauchée or as a third tech (Castle Age at the Castle, before the Chevauchée in Imperial Age)?
4. A third tech at the Market for the Calais Staple (1363), the wool monopoly that paid the Calais garrison (analysis proposal; e.g. ‘Trade Units generate +10% gold’, after the Spanish team bonus ‘Trade Units generate +25% gold’)? The wool trade also fed the cloth towns of Flanders and Italy, so it is a second team-bonus-type trait (RULES 6.2); here wool stays in the Shepherds bonus and the archers carry the team bonus.

### Gaels (Celts in the game) ([Gaels.toml](Gaels.toml))

1. Scope: Gaelic Ireland and Gaelic Scotland only, or also the medieval kingdom of Scotland (from the Canmore kings through Balliol and Bruce to the Stewarts)? This design includes the kingdom. Elements that depend on it: the Schiltron (Falkirk 1298, Bannockburn 1314); the Bombard Cannon (James II); the Knight and Cavalier kept for the Anglo-Norman-descended nobility of Scotland; the siege engines kept in the tree (Trebuchet, Onager, Capped Ram, Siege Tower, Scorpions), since Scottish siege engineering existed, if limited and mostly unsuccessful, while Gaelic Ireland had none to speak of; the stone-castle items (Keep, Arrowslits, Hoardings), which in Gaelic terms would rest only on the West Highland castles and the later tower houses; the Cannon Galleon, which only the ships of James III and James IV could support (Andrew Wood’s Yellow Carvel in the 1480s–90s; the Great Michael of 1511 is after the window), while the Carrack and Galleon stay either way as abstractions under the codex navy rule for fleets of ships; part of the infantry-speed basis (Bruce’s raids of 1314–22); and part of the anti-building basis (the castles taken in 1313–14; it also rests on Irish cases and would stand without Scotland). For inclusion: Bruce’s war had a Gaelic side (he was Earl of Carrick, still largely Gaelic-speaking c. 1300, and Barbour puts Angus Óg of Islay with the men of Carrick, Argyll and the Isles in the king’s own division at Bannockburn). Against: the kingdom’s elite and institutions were largely Anglo-Norman. If it is excluded, the Schiltron and the Bombard Cannon need Irish replacements and the other items above should be reviewed; the Town Center tech (Buannacht) follows the Gaelic lordships either way.
2. Unique units: the Galloglass is the Varangian Guard renamed and trained at the Barracks, a tree unit like the Saxons’ and Danes’ Housecarl, so the civ has one unique unit, the Kern. The model is now fielded by four civs under three names: Varangian Guard (Byzantines), Housecarl (Danes, Saxons) and Galloglass (Gaels); the Norwegians and Swedes drop it. Alternatives: a true second unique unit on the infantry Teutonic Knight model (slow, heavily armoured, strong in melee), which fits the galloglass best and is free if the owner accepts the Germans’ mounted Teutonic Knight; or the Goths’ Huskarl, unused but ‘weak vs. Cavalry’, a poor fit. Note also that the Kern, the cheapest Gaelic soldier, is the Castle unit while the costly galloglass trains at the Barracks (a game convention). Keep the renamed tree unit?
3. Team bonus: the Irish missions now hold it (RULES 6.2, religious influence), with ‘Monasteries work +20% faster’, the same team bonus as the Saxons’ Anglo-Saxon missions and six other designs (Bulgarians, Burmese, Danes, Hindustanis, Khitans, Malay); the reuse itself is codex-wide question CQ11. Alternatives: ‘Universities work +80% faster’ for the Irish scholars of the Carolingian schools (Dicuil, Dungal, Sedulius Scottus, John Scottus Eriugena), which would set the Gaels apart from the Saxons but repeat the team bonus of the Franks and five other designs; or, if the kingdom of Scotland stays in scope, the Auld Alliance with France (Treaty of Paris, 1295; the Scottish army in France, 1419–24, with Baugé, 1421, and Verneuil, 1424; the Scots guard of the French kings). The hobelars are not used for the team bonus: they served the English Crown more than the Gaels’ allies, and they appear as the Hussar renamed Hobelar. Keep the missions?
4. Buannacht reuses Kshatriyas (Military Units cost -25% food; level 1), which renders most literally the food the subjects owed; seven designs, the Gaels included, use that effect (the reuse itself is codex-wide question CQ11). Alternative: a new tech aimed at the hired soldiers alone, ‘Kerns and Galloglasses cost -20%’ (level 2). Keep level 1?
5. Codex-wide question CQ19 (see codex/civs/README.md): Labels (here the game’s ‘Infantry and Siege civilization’ becomes ‘Infantry civilization’).

### Saxons ([Saxons.toml](Saxons.toml))

1. Name (not decided; to be settled with the other open renames, codex-wide question CQ16): keep ‘Saxons’, or ‘Anglo-Saxons’? ‘Saxons’ leaves out the Angles who gave England its name (Bede’s Northumbria, Offa’s Mercia) and collides with the continental Saxons of the Ottonian dynasty; the people called themselves Englisc or Angelcynn. If the Britons become ‘English’, ‘Anglo-Saxons’ keeps the two civs apart.
2. Shield Wall: RULES 6.2 puts a long military tradition in a passive, so the shield wall is now a passive (from the Imperial Age, when the game’s tech came) and the Castle slot goes to a datable reform, Alfred’s divided levy (the Fyrd, the Malay Forced Levy renamed, level 1). The game’s own arrangement, Shield Wall as the Castle tech, could be kept for familiarity, with the Fyrd dropped. Keep the new arrangement?
3. Food bonus: the feorm went to royal vills and estate centres, so the history favours ‘Mills and Town Centers provide +35 food when built’; balance favours the current targets (Mills, Lumber- and Mining Camps), since Town Centers are built rarely and the bonus would lose most of its value in a civ that already loses much of its tree. Which?
4. Codex-wide question CQ5 (see codex/civs/README.md): Architecture (here the game’s Western European set becomes Northern European).
5. Codex-wide question CQ11 (see codex/civs/README.md): Reuse and stacking (here Edgar's Recoinage, Clerical Recruitment, the Fyrd and the team bonus ‘Monasteries work +20% faster’).
6. Codex-wide question CQ13 (see codex/civs/README.md): Long-range siege (without the Trebuchet the Saxons have no long-range siege weapon and rely on the Siege Ram as the RULES 6.11 substitute; the Armenians, Ethiopians, Goths, Khmer, Malay, Malians and Tatars are in the same position).
7. Codex-wide question CQ14 (see codex/civs/README.md): Power floor (the tree loses fifteen items, mostly later than 1066).
8. Codex-wide question CQ25 (see codex/civs/README.md): Rule interpretations, point (a) on the chronological order of unique techs (here the Fyrd, for Alfred’s divided levy of 893, is the Imperial Age tech at the Castle, while Edgar's Recoinage, for the reform of c. 973, and Clerical Recruitment, for the conversion of the Danelaw and the 10th-century Benedictine reform, are Castle Age techs at the Town Center and the Monastery; all three keep the game ages of their effects).

### Franks ([Franks.toml](Franks.toml))

1. Name (not decided; to be settled with the other open renames, codex-wide question CQ16): ‘Franks’ fuses the Frankish kingdoms and medieval France. ‘French’ fits 987–1500 better; ‘Franks’ has a defence in the crusading period, when Byzantines and Muslims called all Latins Franks (Frangoi, Ifranj). Rename, keep, or split (N3 idea)?
2. Castle tech: Chivalry is dropped (RULES 6.2: a structural ethos, whose knightly tradition the cavalry passive already carries) and the Castle slot goes to the Bureau Brothers (Drill renamed, level 1), which also puts the techs in chronological order (1439–45 in Castle Age, 1449–53 in Imperial Age). Keep Chivalry anyway for its fame, as a third tech (Castle Age at the Castle, before the Bureau Brothers)? Separately, the francs-archers (ordinance of 1448: one archer per parish, exempt from the taille) could be a third tech at the Town Center, since they were a parish levy paid by a tax exemption: e.g. ‘Archer-line trainable at Town Centers’ (precedent: ‘Settlements can train Spearman-line and Skirmishers’, Mapuche, stretched to Town Centers), a weak candidate because they were an uneven militia, mocked in the ‘Franc-archer de Bagnolet’ and disbanded after Guinegate (1479). Add either?
3. Team bonus: the University of Paris (Universities work +80% faster) is now the team bonus of six designs (Chinese, Franks, Khmer, Malians, Persians, Poles), and the Georgians’ Gelati Academy contains it; the reuse itself is codex-wide question CQ11. The analysis’ ‘Oriflamme’ fails RULES 6.2, since the royal banner benefited only the French. Other French traits that benefited neighbours and that no other civ claims: the Champagne fairs of the 12th–13th centuries, where Italian and Flemish merchants met (with, e.g., ‘Trade Units generate +10% gold’, although five designs already use that effect and it is close to the Burgundians’ Bruges bonus), or the French knights of the crusades and the Reconquista. Gothic building, a third candidate, is used as a passive for French building at home. Keep the shared University bonus?
4. Power check: the forager bonus and Chivalry are lost; Gothic building (free Masonry and Architecture), the Bureau Brothers, the Keep, Arbalester, Ring Archer Armor and Guilds are gained. Bracer is held back for power: the civ is a cavalry civ, the added Arbalester and Ring Archer Armor already bring its archers close to the archer civs’, and Bracer would also strengthen its cheap castles. Tree similarity (Jaccard, current tree plus additions and removals) goes from 0.821 to 0.851 with the Burgundians and from 0.861 to 0.885 with the Britons (see the next question). Acceptable?
5. Differentiation (region-wide; the owner asked to tell the five civs apart clearly): bonuses, unique units and unique techs differ throughout, but removing anachronisms leaves each tree closer to the generic late-medieval Western tree. Tree similarity (Jaccard on the current tree plus each design’s additions and removals; current → design): Britons–Saxons 0.807 → 0.792; Britons–Gaels 0.824 → 0.859; Britons–Franks 0.861 → 0.885; Britons–Burgundians 0.800 → 0.850; Saxons–Gaels 0.760 → 0.790; Saxons–Franks 0.763 → 0.750; Saxons–Burgundians 0.740 → 0.739; Gaels–Franks 0.811 → 0.825; Gaels–Burgundians 0.764 → 0.780; Franks–Burgundians 0.821 → 0.851. Each generic item moves a pair by about 0.006: the Britons’ Crop Rotation, the Burgundians’ loss of Incendiaries and the Saxons’ Carrack (kept under the codex navy rule) raise several pairs, and leaving Ring Archer Armor out of the Burgundians lowers their pairs with the Britons and Franks (0.856 and 0.857 with it). Trim further, for example the Franks’ Ring Archer Armor (Britons–Franks 0.879) or the Gaels’ Bombard Cannon if the kingdom of Scotland leaves the scope?
6. Codex-wide question CQ4 (see codex/civs/README.md): Mule Cart (no civ of this region has it).

### Burgundians ([Burgundians.toml](Burgundians.toml))

1. Town Center tech: the common coinage of 1433–34 (Vierlander Coinage, Grand Trunk Road renamed, level 1) now holds the state slot, and Burgundian Vineyards moves to the Mill, since the 1395 ordinance concerns farming and weighs less for the state than the ducal institutions. The coinage’s effect is shared with five other designs (the Saxons’ Edgar's Recoinage, the Hindustanis’ Tanka, the Khitans’ Chanyuan Treaty, the Malay Syahbandar and the Muisca Cotton Mantles; the reuse itself is codex-wide question CQ11), and the civ now has three techs, two of them economic. Alternatives: a level-2 tech for another institution with an effect no one else uses (the Chambres des comptes of 1386 or the Parlement of Mechelen of 1473), or Vineyards at the Town Center and no third tech. Which?
2. Imperial unique tech: the Order of the Golden Fleece (a new tech, level 2) replaces Flemish Revolution, with armour for the nobility the Order headed and resistance to conversion for its knights’ sworn loyalty to the duke. Its armour half is Hauberk’s (Sicilians; the Armenians’ Sparapet) at the +1/+1 magnitude of Silk Armor (Tatars), so that the Paladin stays at 6/8, within the game’s current Knight-line maximum (the default of codex-wide question CQ9). Alternatives: Hauberk shared and renamed ‘Order of the Golden Fleece’ (level 1, armour only, the half least specific to the Order; it would give a 6/9 Paladin, above that ceiling), or ‘Great Bombards’ for the ducal artillery park (Bombard Cannons +1 range, after the Turks’ Artillery at a lower magnitude; Siege Weapons +20% attack vs. buildings, after Siege Engineers). Which?
3. Cavalier upgrade in Castle Age is kept as the mark of a lavishly equipped tournament nobility, although it is rated ‘stretched’ (the Burgundian standing companies of 1471 came 26 years after the French ones of 1445) and overlaps the Golden Fleece’s armour half. Keep it, or replace it?
4. Differentiation from the Franks and the Britons: the mounted-crossbow line stays Frankish (with their Ordonnance Companies tech), so the Burgundians keep only the Mounted Crossbowman, without Heavy Mounted Crossbowman and Cranequins; and Ring Archer Armor is not added, so that the archer discount does not come with the Britons’ full archery upgrades. Tree similarity with the Franks goes from 0.821 to 0.851 and with the Britons from 0.800 to 0.850 (0.856 with Ring Archer Armor); the Franks’ file lists all region pairs. Add any of these?
5. English archers: Charles the Bold hired English archers after his alliance with Edward IV and his marriage to Margaret of York (1468); they served at Neuss and Morat (Vaughan 1973). A tech letting the civ train Longbowmen (analysis proposal) is not adopted, to keep the Longbowman distinct to the Britons. Agree?
6. Power check: the civ loses the stable discount, Flemish Revolution, the relic team bonus, Hussar, Siphons and Incendiaries, and gains the archer discount, the common coinage (a third tech), the Golden Fleece, the trade team bonus and three tree items (Arbalester, Siege Engineers, Dry Dock): a shift from cavalry towards archers, siege and economy, with three economic elements (the early economic upgrades, the Vineyards and the coinage). Acceptable?
7. Flemish Militia keeps its name although the Flemish towns fought the dukes more often than for them (Gavere, 1453). Keep it?
8. Codex-wide question CQ19 (see codex/civs/README.md): Labels (here the game’s ‘Cavalry civilization’ becomes ‘Cavalry and Gunpowder civilization’).
9. Codex-wide question CQ25 (see codex/civs/README.md): Rule interpretations, point (a) on the chronological order of unique techs (here the Order of the Golden Fleece, founded in 1430, is the Imperial Age tech at the Castle, in the slot of Flemish Revolution, while Vierlander Coinage, for the common coinage of 1433–34, is a Castle Age tech at the Town Center, the game age of Grand Trunk Road).

## Iberia and Italy

### Castilians (Spanish in the game) ([Castilians.toml](Castilians.toml))

1. Castle tech: Órdenes Militares strengthens the Freires, so the military orders fill two high slots (the unit and the tech); its +20 HP also lifts the Elite Freire to 100 HP, above the game's Elite Monaspa (80), a case for codex-wide question CQ9 (magnitude ceiling). History favours a distinct datable moment for the tech (RULES 6.3 and 6.9). Options: (a) keep Órdenes Militares (Castle Age; it fits your 'Cavalry and Monk' label); (b) a Castle Age tech for the cuantía laws, which obliged men of a set wealth to keep horse and arms (caballeros de cuantía, 14th–15th centuries), for example a Knight-line discount (another gold or resource saving, see the power question); (c) an Imperial tech for the royal artillery train of the war of Granada under Francisco Ramírez de Madrid, whose guns reached Cambil and Alhabar in 1485 along a road cut through the sierra (Pulgar), e.g. 'Bombard Cannons move +25% faster' (precedent: Drill, 'Siege Workshop Units move +50% faster', Mongols); all three techs would then fall in 1476–1492, and the Granada guns would fill two slots (this tech and the gunpowder passive); (d) an Imperial tech for the Guardas Reales (1493), Castile's first standing royal force; (e) if the orders keep the tech, a separate moment for it: the crown's assumption of their masterships (Calatrava 1487–1489, Santiago 1493, Alcántara 1494). Recommended if you want a distinct moment: (b), which keeps a Castle Age cavalry tech within the label; (c) is the weightiest but would make the civ's techs all late. Which?
2. Name of the order knight (Monaspa model): 'Freire', the orders' own word for a professed brother, or 'Caballero de Calatrava' / 'Caballero de Santiago'?
3. Builders (on your list) is dropped for relevance (RULES 6.9): the repoblación it was meant to carry is central, but building speed expresses it weakly. The repoblación gets 'Town Centers cost -50% wood starting in Castle Age' instead (the Britons' bonus, there for planted boroughs). Keep this, restore the builders, or use the slot for the Castilian galleys ('Galley-line +1 attack': Seville's royal shipyard, 1252; the Boccanegra admirals; La Rochelle, 1372; the raids on the English coast in the 1370s–1380s)? Composition is 6 passive bonuses + 3 unique techs = 9, at the proposed cap (the Mule Cart is not given: as a unique building it would count as a seventh passive, RULES 6.1).
4. Mesta: the passive is 'Shepherding also generates gold' (wool sold abroad) rather than 'Shepherds work +25% faster' (Britons), which the Berbers', Britons', Bulgarians' and Norwegians' designs already use (and the Inca's within a combined bonus). With the Norwegians' and Tamils' 'Fishing Ships generate gold in addition to food' and the Swedes' 'Hunting also generates gold', it completes the split of the game's Varangian bonus. The same wool also underlies the team bonus, seen from the buyers' side. Keep both, or separate them: the passive for the transhumance with a shepherding effect, the team bonus for the export trade, or a team bonus on another benefit to allies (see the next question)?
5. Team bonus: the game's 'Trade Units generate +25% gold' is kept for the wool trade, but the Mongols' design also uses it as its team bonus, and its wool repeats the Mesta passive. Identical team-wide effects count once per team, and trade-gold team bonuses count as one at the highest value present (codex rule pending the owner; codex-wide question CQ11), so a Castilian–Mongol team gains nothing from the copy; as the game's original holder, the Castilians have the better claim to it. Alternatives that benefited neighbours: (a) Alfonsine science and the Toledo translations (Archbishop Raymond, 1125–1152; Gerard of Cremona's Almagest, 1175; Michael Scot, c. 1217; the Alfonsine Tables of c. 1272, used across Europe and printed in 1483), e.g. 'Technologies research +25% faster', which is the Saracens' team bonus and whose basis already counts the Toledo translations among the channels of Arabic science, or a University effect ('Universities work +80% faster' is already the team bonus of six designs); a University tech for the Alfonsine Tables is another route, but at 6 + 3 = 9 a passive would have to go; (b) the Castilian galleys that served France under the alliance of Toledo (1368): La Rochelle (1372), the raids on the English coast in the 1370s–1380s and Pero Niño's Channel campaign (1405), with a naval team effect. Keep the wool trade, or switch?
6. Bula de Cruzada: Imperial Age, for Sixtus IV's bull of 1482, since your decision ties it to the war of Granada. Crusade privileges for the Spanish wars began much earlier (indulgences from the 12th century; the tercias reales granted for the conquest of Seville, 1247), so a Castle Age tech is also defensible. Imperial or Castle Age?
7. Codex-wide question CQ4 (see codex/civs/README.md): Mule Cart.
8. Codex-wide question CQ17 (see codex/civs/README.md): Renames of shared generic items.
9. Window: the civ starts in 1037, when Ferdinand I joined León to Castile; the county of Castile (Fernán González, from the 930s) and the kingdom of León (from c. 910) appear only as background in the bases (the charter of 800, the Duero repoblación, Compostela). Start the window earlier (910 or the 930s)?
10. Power: compared with the game's Spanish, the civ gains a sixth passive, a third unique tech, the Crossbowman, Arbalester, Siege Engineers and the Jinete line, and keeps the Paladin, Hussar, Heavy Mounted Crossbowman, Hand Cannoneer, Bombard Cannon, Siege Ram and the faster gunpowder fire; it loses the builders, +20 gold per technology, the Cannon Galleon accuracy and its two unique units, and, under codex-wide criteria, Thumb Ring (European draw and crossbows, see the tree; its accuracy effect also leaves the Jinete line) and Siphons (no ship-borne liquid fire). Gold comes or is saved through Blacksmith upgrades without gold, the Bula (-15% gold for military units), shepherding gold and a free Relic. The Mule Cart stays out (codex-wide question CQ4). Further counterweights if needed: a lower Bula discount (-10%), or no Heavy Mounted Crossbowman. Accept as is?
11. Santa Hermandad: the tech keeps your name and effect, but the example in RULES 6.3, which presents it as the formalisation of the municipal militias, is historically loose: the Hermandad of 1476 was a crown-controlled police and fiscal-military league of the towns that grew out of the medieval brotherhoods (hermandades), while the fuero militias were an older, separate institution whose town contingents served beside it in the war of Granada. The basis says so; you may want to reword the RULES example.

### Portuguese ([Portuguese.toml](Portuguese.toml))

1. Team bonus: 'Ships move +5% faster' for allies, for the captains, pilots, cosmographers and charts that carried Portuguese navigation to Castile and Italy (the Atlantic wind system itself belongs to Volta do Mar, and the caravel to the unique unit). Alternatives: 'Docks +6 line of sight' (the game's Malay team bonus, which the Malay design replaces, so no design uses it) or 'Docks cost -15%' (the Vikings' team bonus, which the Norwegians' design keeps). Which do you prefer?
2. Aljubarrota's effect: armour for Infantry and Foot Archers (the present effect, for the prepared defensive position of Atoleiros and Aljubarrota), or the narrower anti-cavalry 'Spearman-line and Foot Archers receive -2 damage from Mounted Units' (precedent: Royal Heirs, Ethiopians), which fits Atoleiros best?
3. The Besteiro do Conto unit and the free crossbow upgrades both rest on the crossbowmen, as your example and your decision imply. Keep both, or replace the passive with another trait?
4. The Besteiro do Conto shares the Genoese Crossbowman model with the Italians, whose team bonus makes the Genoese Crossbowman available to allies: a Portuguese–Italian team would field the same unit under two names. Acceptable?
5. Ordem de Cristo's relic effect (your decision) keeps the Lithuanian maximum of +4 but applies it to units with far lower base attack. The game's Lithuanian bonus (which the Lithuanians' design replaces) adds 25–40% to the Knight-line and Leitis (base attack 10–16); on Portuguese ships +4 adds 44–67% to the Galley line (6/7/9), 57–100% to the Caravel (4, Elite 7), 100% to the Hulk line (4) and 133–200% to the Fire Galley and Fire Ship (2–3, firing every 0.25 s), and it stacks with the ship-HP passive, Volta do Mar's faster ship production, the team's faster ships and the tech's own Dock discount, from the Castle Age. At +4 it also exceeds the highest attack bonus the game gives the Galley line (+2, the Romans' Ballistas), a case for codex-wide question CQ9 (magnitude ceiling). Keep it, or narrow it: Galley line and Caravels only, a maximum of +2, or +1 per two Relics?
6. Power and the Reconquest kingdom: 4 passive bonuses + 4 unique techs = 8. Without the -20% gold and forager-wood bonuses the land economy has no bonus before Lei das Sesmarias (Castle Age), and no element stands for the kingdom's first century (Santarém and Lisbon 1147, Alcácer do Sal 1217, the Algarve 1249); the castle network of D. Dinis is already in the tree (Arrowslits, Hoardings). A fifth passive (9, the cap) could answer both: the military orders (Templars, Hospitallers, Santiago, Avis) that held the Tagus and Alentejo frontier; the concelho cavalry of the forais (cavaleiros-vilãos); or the Mule Cart for the almocreves (codex-wide question CQ4), which as a unique building would count as this passive (RULES 6.1). Under codex-wide criteria the tree also loses Thumb Ring (European draw and crossbows, see the tree; its accuracy effect also leaves the Ginete line) and Siphons (no ship-borne liquid fire). Accept 8, or add one (which)?
7. Codex-wide question CQ4 (see codex/civs/README.md): Mule Cart.
8. Shipwright (an N1 tree addition, not N2): the Portuguese lack it, which the tech tree audit calls ironic for a seafaring civ (the royal shipyard of Lisbon could be its basis). Add it, perhaps lowering the ship HP bonus, since it would stack with Volta do Mar's faster ship production?

### Italians ([Italians.toml](Italians.toml))

1. Town Center tech: 'Comune' reuses Tigui (Town Centers fire arrows without garrison, level 1) for the commune that defended itself with its own sworn citizens. The Malians' design drops Tigui, but the Germans' design also reuses it, as Burgenordnung for Henry I's burgs, so two neighbouring civs share the same Town Center tech. History favours the commune here: it is the defining Italian institution, while the Germans' design already offers the Golden Bull (1356) as a stronger state moment for theirs, so if only one civ keeps the effect, the Italians have the better claim. Alternatives for the Italians: a fiscal institution as a level-2 tech, the funded public debt (Venice's Monte, 1262; Florence's Monte, 1345), the Florentine Catasto (1427) or Genoa's Casa di San Giorgio (1407), with 'All gold income +10% faster' (already in ten designs), at the price of an even stronger economy; the Podestà; or Commenda moved to the Town Center (level 1). Keep Comune and settle the duplicate on the German side, or switch?
2. Heavy cavalry and power: the Condotta gives the Knight-line +50% training speed (level 2, from Comitatenses), a targeted expression of the lances; Chivalry (Stables work +40% faster, the Franks' tech in the game, which their design drops) is not used because it would also speed the Stradiots, Light Cavalry and Mounted Crossbowmen. The Paladin and the Siege Ram stay out as counterweights to the stronger economy (Market technologies free), the new Halberdier, Siege Engineers and Town Center tech, although lances in Milanese plate and the communal sieges of the 12th–13th centuries (Crema, 1159–1160) could justify them; in the Imperial Age (the 15th century for this civ) Italian siegecraft relied on bombards, which the civ has. Other options: a Knight-line passive (for example Knight-line armour for Milanese plate) in place of the gunpowder passive, the weakest military passive; or a unit on the Burgundian Coustillier model ('Lancia', N1), though the coustillier was the lightly armed attendant of the man-at-arms, so the model would invert the lance. Under codex-wide criteria the tree also loses Thumb Ring (European draw and crossbows, see the tree), Siphons (no ship-borne liquid fire) and Incendiaries, which requires Siphons.
